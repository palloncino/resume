01 Fundamental Quantities

Current

I = Q / t
“I equals Q divided by t.”
Q = I × t
“Q equals I times t.”
I = current (A) · Q = charge (C) · t = time (s) · 1 A = 1 C/s

Voltage

V = E / Q
“V equals E divided by Q.”
E = V × Q
“E equals V times Q.”
V = volts · E = energy (J) · Q = coulombs · 1 V = 1 J/C
Voltage drop is the difference in electrical potential energy per coulomb across an element. Loads transfer energy — they do not consume charge.

Power & Energy

P = E / t
“P equals E divided by t.”
E = P × t
“E equals P times t.”
1 W = 1 J/s
P = V × I
“P equals V times I.”
I = P/V   ·   V = P/I
Rearranged forms of P = V × I.

02 Ohm's Law

V = I × R
“V equals I times R.”
I = V / R
“I equals V divided by R.”
R = V / I
“R equals V divided by I.”
V = volts · I = amperes · R = ohms (Ω)

03 Resistive Power / Heating

P = I² × R
“P equals I squared times R.”
P = V² / R
“P equals V squared divided by R.”
Also P = V × I — see “Fundamental Quantities”.
Cable heating rises with the square of current.

04 Series Circuits

Resistance

Rtotal = R1 + R2 + R3 + …
“Total resistance equals R one plus R two plus R three, and so on.”

Voltage

Vtotal = V1 + V2 + V3 + …
“Total voltage equals V one plus V two plus V three, and so on.”

Current

I1 = I2 = I3 = Itotal
“The current is the same through every component in series.”

Voltage divider

Vx = Vs × (Rx / Rtotal)
“V x equals supply voltage times R x divided by total resistance.”
Series: same current, voltage divides.

05 Parallel Circuits

Voltage

V1 = V2 = V3 = Vsupply
“Each branch voltage equals the supply voltage.”

Current — Kirchhoff's Current Law

Itotal = I1 + I2 + I3 + …
“Total current equals the sum of the branch currents.”

Resistance

1/Rtotal = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + 1/R3 + …
“One divided by total resistance equals the sum of one divided by each branch resistance.”
Rtotal = (R1 × R2) / (R1 + R2)
“Total resistance equals R one times R two divided by R one plus R two.” (two resistors)
Parallel: same voltage, current divides.

06 Kirchhoff's Laws

Current law — KCL

Σ Iin = Σ Iout
“The sum of currents entering equals the sum of currents leaving.”

Voltage law — KVL

Σ V = 0
“The algebraic sum of voltages around a closed loop equals zero.”
Vsupply = Vdrop1 + Vdrop2 + …
Practical form of KVL.

07 Cable Resistance

R = ρ × L / A
“R equals rho times L divided by A.”
R = resistance · ρ (rho) = material resistivity · L = conductor length · A = cross-sectional area
Rloop = 2 × ρ × L / A
“Loop resistance equals two times rho times L divided by A.”
Only use the factor 2 when L is the one-way length.
Longer cable → more resistance. Larger cross-sectional area → less resistance.

08 Voltage Drop & Cable Loss

ΔV = I × R
“Voltage drop equals current times resistance.”
Voltage drop % = (ΔV / Vsupply) × 100
“Voltage-drop percentage equals voltage drop divided by supply voltage times one hundred.”
ΔV = (mV/A/m × I × L) / 1000
“Voltage drop equals millivolts per amp per metre times current times length divided by one thousand.”
Use the tabulated mV/A/m value from the applicable cable/design tables.
Ploss = I² × R
“Power loss equals current squared times cable resistance.”

09 Electrical Energy / Billing

Energy (kWh) = Power (kW) × Time (h)
“Energy in kilowatt-hours equals power in kilowatts times time in hours.”
Cost = kWh × tariff
“Cost equals kilowatt-hours times the electricity tariff.”
1000 W = 1 kW · 1000 Wh = 1 kWh

10 Efficiency

η = Pout / Pin
“Eta equals output power divided by input power.”
η% = (Pout / Pin) × 100
“Efficiency percentage equals output power divided by input power times one hundred.”
η (eta) = efficiency

11 AC Fundamentals

Frequency & period

f = 1 / T
“Frequency equals one divided by period.”
T = 1 / f
“Period equals one divided by frequency.”
f = frequency, hertz (Hz) · T = period (s)
50 Hz → T = 0.02 s = 20 ms

Angular frequency

ω = 2πf
“Omega equals two pi times frequency.”
ω (omega) = angular frequency

Sine-wave voltage

v(t) = Vpeak × sin(2πft)
“Voltage at time t equals peak voltage times sine of two pi f t.”

12 RMS & Peak AC Values

Vrms = Vpeak / √2
“RMS voltage equals peak voltage divided by square root of two.”
Vpeak = Vrms × √2
“Peak voltage equals RMS voltage times square root of two.”
Irms = Ipeak / √2   ·   Ipeak = Irms × √2
Same relationship applies to current.
230 V RMS ≈ 325 V peak

13 Capacitors

Q = C × V
“Charge equals capacitance times voltage.”
C = capacitance, farads (F)
E = ½ C V²
“Energy equals one half C times V squared.”
XC = 1 / (2πfC)
“X C equals one divided by two pi f C.”
Higher frequency → lower capacitive reactance.

14 Inductors

V = L × (ΔI / Δt)
“Voltage equals inductance times change in current divided by change in time.”
E = ½ L I²
“Energy equals one half L times I squared.”
XL = 2πfL
“X L equals two pi f L.”
Higher frequency → higher inductive reactance.

15 Impedance

Z = V / I   ·   I = V / Z
“Impedance equals voltage divided by current.”
Z = √(R² + (XL − XC)²)
“Impedance equals the square root of R squared plus X L minus X C squared.” (series R-L-C)
Z = impedance (Ω) · R = resistance · XL = inductive reactance · XC = capacitive reactance

16 AC Power

S = V × I
“S equals V times I.” — apparent power (VA)
P = V × I × cos φ
“P equals V times I times cosine phi.” — real power (W)
Qreactive = V × I × sin φ
“Reactive power equals V times I times sine phi.” (var)
S² = P² + Qreactive²
Power triangle.
PF = cos φ = P / S
“Power factor equals cosine phi, which equals real power divided by apparent power.”
φ (phi) = phase angle between V and I
Purely resistive load: power factor ≈ 1, so P ≈ V × I.

17 Transformers

Vp / Vs = Np / Ns
“Primary voltage divided by secondary voltage equals primary turns divided by secondary turns.”
Ip / Is = Ns / Np
“Primary current divided by secondary current equals secondary turns divided by primary turns.”
Vp × Ip ≈ Vs × Is
“Primary voltage times primary current approximately equals secondary voltage times secondary current.”
p = primary · s = secondary · N = number of turns

18 Single-Phase Power

P = V × I
Resistive load.
P = V × I × PF
“Power equals voltage times current times power factor.” (general AC load)

19 Three-Phase Power

P = √3 × VL × IL × cos φ
“Power equals square root of three times line voltage times line current times cosine phi.”
S = √3 × VL × IL
“S equals square root of three times line voltage times line current.”
Qreactive = √3 × VL × IL × sin φ
“Reactive power equals square root of three times line voltage times line current times sine phi.”
VL = line-to-line voltage · IL = line current
EU 230/400 V system: Line–Neutral ≈ 230 V · Line–Line ≈ 400 V

20 Star & Delta

Star / Wye

VL = √3 × Vphase
“Line voltage equals square root of three times phase voltage.”
IL = Iphase
“Line current equals phase current.”

Delta

VL = Vphase
“Line voltage equals phase voltage.”
IL = √3 × Iphase
“Line current equals square root of three times phase current.”

21 RCD / Residual Current

IΔ = |IL − IN|
“Residual current equals the absolute difference between Live current and Neutral current.”
IΔ = residual/differential current · IL = Live current · IN = Neutral current
e.g. 5.000 A − 4.975 A = 0.025 A = 25 mA
MCB → excessive current. RCD → current imbalance.

22 Fault Current / Earth-Fault Loop

Ifault = U0 / Zs
“Fault current equals voltage to earth divided by earth-fault-loop impedance.”
Zs = Ze + (R1 + R2)
“Z s equals Z e plus R one plus R two.”
Zs ≤ U0 / Ia
“Z s must be less than or equal to U zero divided by the current required to operate the protective device.”
U0 = nominal voltage to earth · Ze = external earth-loop impedance · R1 = line conductor resistance · R2 = protective conductor resistance
Actual permitted Zs and disconnection times must always come from the applicable regulations/device tables.

23 Basic Circuit Design Relationship

Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz
“I b is less than or equal to I n, which is less than or equal to I z.”
Ib = design/load current · In = protective-device nominal rating · Iz = cable current-carrying capacity
load → protective device → cable

24 Protective Conductor (Adiabatic)

ADVANCED — use with applicable regulations/tables
S = √(I²t) / k
“S equals the square root of I squared t divided by k.”
S = I√t / k
Equivalent form.
S = required conductor cross-sectional area · I = fault current · t = protective-device disconnection time · k = material/insulation constant

25 Motor Basics

Synchronous speed

ns = 120f / p
“Synchronous speed equals one hundred twenty times frequency divided by number of poles.”
ns = synchronous speed (rpm) · f = frequency (Hz) · p = number of poles

Induction-motor slip

Slip % = ((ns − nr) / ns) × 100
“Slip percentage equals synchronous speed minus rotor speed, divided by synchronous speed, times one hundred.”
nr = actual rotor speed

Mechanical power

Pmechanical = τ × ω
“Mechanical power equals torque times angular velocity.”
τ (tau) = torque (N·m) · ω (omega) = angular velocity (rad/s)

Mental model — hold onto these

Current: charge per second.
Voltage: potential-energy difference per coulomb.
Resistance: opposition to current.
Power: rate of energy transfer.
Load: transfers electrical energy; it does not consume charge.
Series: same current, voltage divides.
Parallel: same voltage, current divides.
MCB: protects against overcurrent.
RCD: detects Live–Neutral current imbalance.
PE: provides a protective fault-current path.
Neutral: normally near earth potential; not "used-up Live".
Ib≤In≤Iz: load → protective device → cable.
